earnrate

Privacy

earnrate needs no account, no email and no card numbers. On earnrate.dev and on your computer it runs on your own device. In Claude it runs on earnrate's server, which answers without keeping your questions, and keeps an anonymous wallet, started when you connect it and holding cards only if you save some, encrypted so that only your recovery code or the apps you connect can open it. Here's where your data goes, and where it doesn't.

Updated November 6, 2026.

On earnrate.dev

Everything on earnrate.dev runs in your browser: the card comparison, the spending simulator and your wallet. The card data loads with the page, and the stores, amounts, cards and settings you enter are never sent anywhere, except when you add the cheat sheet to Apple Wallet or turn on syncing your wallet with Claude (both below). The site does count which of its parts get used, by name only (also below). Your wallet, your spending amounts and your settings are saved in your browser's storage on that device, so they're there next time. Deleting the wallet on the page, or clearing the site's data in your browser, removes them. An exported wallet file goes only where you save it.

A store you search for appears in the address after a #. Browsers don't send that part to the server, and Cloudflare's analytics doesn't record it. If you share the link, whoever opens it sees the search.

So the page works without a connection, for example after you add it to your phone's Home Screen, your browser also keeps a copy of earnrate.dev's own files (the page, its code and the card data) and checks for a newer one when you're online. It holds nothing you typed, and clearing the site's data removes it.

The Apple Wallet pass

Only when you press Add to Apple Wallet, your browser sends earnrate's pass server (pass.earnrate.dev) what the cheat sheet needs: your cards by product, like amex-gold, their category picks and activations, any checkout names you gave them, your memberships and today's date. No spending amounts, searches or card numbers. The server works out the cheat sheet, signs the pass so Apple Wallet accepts it, and sends it back. It doesn't keep what you sent or the pass, request logging is turned off, and Cloudflare limits how many passes one IP address can make per minute. It counts passes made and refused, with the kind of device, and nothing about the pass.

The pass then lives in Apple Wallet on your device, under Apple's privacy terms. It doesn't check back with earnrate for updates: when the quarter ends it expires, and you make a new one.

If you sync with Claude

Syncing is off unless you turn it on in My wallet. With it on, earnrate.dev uses the same saved wallet as earnrate in Claude. You sign in on earnrate's sign-in page, the one Claude opens, by starting a wallet or typing your recovery code there. Your wallet then goes to earnrate's server at mcp.earnrate.dev and is stored encrypted, like the connector's (see Your saved wallet, below): your cards, their picks and activations, memberships, point values, cap and spending totals, statement credits used and the ones you don't use, opening dates, and any card issuers you've left out of next-card answers. A purchase you record is sent so its totals can be updated; only the totals are kept. Searches, stores and amounts you ask about still never leave your browser: answers are still worked out on the page.

The sign-in is kept in a cookie that your browser sends only to earnrate.dev's own sync addresses (under /api) and that the page's scripts can't read. earnrate.dev's server uses it to pass your wallet and its changes to mcp.earnrate.dev, and stores nothing itself. Stopping syncing signs this browser out and keeps a copy of the wallet here; changing your recovery code or deleting the wallet signs it out too. Using the wallet from the site counts as use, so a synced wallet is still deleted only after 13 months unused.

What the site counts

So I can tell which parts of earnrate.dev people use, the page counts the actions below. When you do one, your browser sends earnrate.dev just that action's name, at most once per visit, and the server adds one to a tally along with the kind of device: iPhone, Android, computer or other, worked out from your browser's description of itself, which isn't kept. Nothing else is sent: never a store you searched, a card, an amount or a setting. No cookie, ID or IP address is kept, so the tallies can't tell visitors apart or follow anyone. To count how many people start a wallet, your browser's storage remembers that your first card was already counted; that note never leaves your device. Cloudflare limits how many counts one IP address can send per minute.

  • Opening a tab: tab-best, tab-spending, tab-wallet
  • Running a search (not what it was): search; opening the example ranking: example-search; changing an amount in My spending: spending-edit
  • In My wallet: wallet-first-card (the first card ever added in this browser), wallet-card-added, wallet-which-card (asking which card to use), wallet-export, wallet-import, cheat-sheet (the cheat sheet was shown), cheat-sheet-image (downloaded as an image), apple-wallet (pressed Add to Apple Wallet), wallet-two-years (opened the first-two-years view of Would another card help?), wallet-skip-issuers (changed which issuers to leave out, not which ones), wallet-sync-on (pressed Use the same wallet as Claude or Connect again), wallet-sync-off (pressed Stop syncing)
  • Following a link to set up earnrate elsewhere: cta-claude, cta-after-answer, cta-save-cards, cta-pass-hint, cta-bottom
  • Arriving from a button on a card, store or comparison page, counted when the app opens (the card or store itself isn't sent): page-spending (to My spending), page-whatif (to what a card would add), page-settings (to a held card's Settings), page-add (adding a card), page-check (checking a store with your own cards)
  • Following a home-page link for choosing a card: decide-fee, decide-compare, decide-quarterly, decide-best
  • In the setup section: setup-code, setup-desktop, setup-cli (opening those tabs), copy-connector, copy-plugin, copy-cli, copy-cli-mcp (copying a command), desktop-download

In Claude, through earnrate's server

When you add earnrate to Claude as a connector, Claude sends each question it asks earnrate to earnrate's server at mcp.earnrate.dev: the store or kind of purchase, and whatever else you've given for that question, like an amount, the country you're paying in, your monthly spending, the cards you named or your bonus picks and memberships. The server works out the answer and sends it back. It doesn't keep the question or the answer, and request logging is turned off.

It counts how often each kind of request is made, like "best card for a purchase," and how often one fails, by tool or page, without what was asked. Each count also notes the kind of app it came from: Claude, ChatGPT, earnrate.dev, an app on your own computer, or another, judged from the address the app signs in through. It also counts how many wallets are started and how many are used each day, without saying which. To stop abuse, Cloudflare, which runs the server, limits how many requests one IP address can make per minute, and once you're signed in, how many your wallet's connections can make per minute. That limit counts under a one-way hash of the wallet's ID, not the ID itself. earnrate doesn't store IP addresses or those hashes.

Your saved wallet

Adding earnrate to Claude opens an earnrate page where you start a wallet, or reconnect one you have. There's no email and no name. The wallet can stay empty: earnrate then answers from every card, or from the cards you name in the conversation. You get a recovery code, shown once, which is the only way to connect the wallet from another app or device. You type it only on that earnrate page, never in the chat. earnrate can't look it up or reset it.

The wallet holds the cards you saved, by product (like amex-gold), with what you've told earnrate about them: a checkout name, bonus picks, activated offers, memberships, the card's anniversary and opening date, how much of each spending cap you've used, your progress toward a welcome bonus, your spending this year on a card with a yearly spend bonus, the statement credits you've enrolled in, how much of each you've used and which ones you don't use, whether you use a card's yearly free night and what it's worth to you if you've said, and point values if you've set your own. Recording a purchase adds to those totals; the purchase itself isn't kept. earnrate never asks for card numbers, expiry dates, security codes or bank logins, and won't save a checkout name that looks like a card number.

Each wallet is encrypted with its own key. That key can be unlocked only with your recovery code or by an app you connected with it, so the stored wallets can't be read without one of them, including by me. earnrate's server unlocks a wallet only in memory, while answering one of your connected apps or changing your recovery code. Its code is open source, on GitHub. Cloudflare stores the encrypted wallet. The record that an app is connected, which app and when, is kept in Cloudflare's storage until that connection is unused for 90 days; its access tokens are stored only in a form that can't be turned back into the token. When you sign an app out, the wallet keeps a one-way hash of that connection's id, so the app is refused at once, until the connection would have expired anyway.

You can export the wallet as a file that works with earnrate.dev and the command line, or delete it. Deleting it is immediate and signs out every app connected to it. A wallet that goes unused for 13 months is deleted automatically (any question counts as use), and a new one that's never used is deleted after a week. You can change your recovery code at mcp.earnrate.dev/change-code; the old code stops working and connected apps are signed out.

On your computer

Your wallet is a file you control, ~/.earnrate/wallet.yaml unless you point EARNRATE_WALLET somewhere else. earnrate reads it, and writes to it only when you change your cards or settings, record a purchase, or apply an imported statement. Cards are named by product, like amex-gold. earnrate never asks for card numbers, expiry dates, security codes or bank logins.

When you import a statement, earnrate reads the file on your computer and keeps only running totals toward caps, welcome bonuses and yearly spend bonuses, plus one-way fingerprints of the transactions so a second import doesn't count them twice. It doesn't keep a copy of the statement.

When you ask about a store earnrate doesn't recognize, the command line and the local MCP server add its name to ~/.earnrate/missed-merchants.json, with how often you asked and any category given, so you can see which stores earnrate is missing (earnrate missed). It stays on your computer; set EARNRATE_MISSED_MERCHANTS=off to keep no list. The hosted server and earnrate.dev keep no such list.

There's no telemetry. earnrate doesn't report usage, errors or anything else back to me.

The AI assistant you ask

Whether earnrate runs on your computer or on earnrate's server, the assistant's provider receives your conversation, including what earnrate tells the assistant: the cards it compared, its answer, and what's left on your caps. That's covered by the provider's privacy policy, not this one.

The optional merchant lookup

It's off by default. If you turn it on, earnrate sends the name of a store it doesn't recognize to Anthropic's API, using your own API key, to ask what kind of store it is. Only the name you typed is sent: no amounts, cards or wallet. Answers are saved on your computer, in ~/.earnrate/merchant-cache.json, so the same name isn't sent twice. Anthropic handles the request under your agreement with them.

Downloading earnrate

When you install earnrate, npm and GitHub see the download, as they do for any package, under their own privacy policies. I can see npm's public download counts, but nothing about who downloaded it.

This website

earnrate.dev is a static site. Its only server code adds up the counts described above and, if you turn on syncing with Claude, passes your wallet to earnrate's server; that sign-in cookie is its only cookie. It counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, a small script that records which page you opened, the site that linked you here, how fast the page loaded, and your country, browser and device type. It doesn't recognize you from one visit to the next. Cloudflare hosts the site and, like any host, handles your IP address and request details to serve the page and block abuse. Its dashboard shows me aggregate counts, not who visited. Logging is off. The sign-in pages on mcp.earnrate.dev don't load the analytics script.
