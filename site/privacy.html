Privacy

earnrate runs on your own device: in your browser on earnrate.dev, or as software on your computer. There's no account and no service behind it, so there's nothing for me to collect. Here's where your data goes, and where it doesn't.

Updated October 30, 2026.

On earnrate.dev

The card comparison and the spending simulator run entirely in your browser. The card data loads with the page, and the stores, amounts and settings you enter are never sent anywhere. Your spending amounts and settings are saved in your browser's storage on that device, so they're there next time; clearing the site's data in your browser deletes them.

A store you search for appears in the address after a #. Browsers don't send that part to the server, and Cloudflare's analytics doesn't record it. If you share the link, whoever opens it sees the search.

On your computer

Your wallet is a file you control, ~/.earnrate/wallet.yaml unless you point EARNRATE_WALLET somewhere else. earnrate reads it, and writes to it only when you record a purchase or apply an imported statement. Cards are named by product, like amex-gold. earnrate never asks for card numbers, expiry dates, security codes or bank logins.

When you import a statement, earnrate reads the file on your computer and keeps only running totals toward caps and welcome bonuses, plus one-way fingerprints of the transactions so a second import doesn't count them twice. It doesn't keep a copy of the statement.

There's no telemetry. earnrate doesn't report usage, errors or anything else back to me.

When you ask through an AI assistant

If you connect earnrate to Claude or another assistant, earnrate still runs on your computer. But the assistant's provider receives your conversation, including what earnrate tells the assistant: the cards it compared, its answer, and what's left on your caps. That's covered by the provider's privacy policy, not this one.

The optional merchant lookup

It's off by default. If you turn it on, earnrate sends the name of a store it doesn't recognize to Anthropic's API, using your own API key, to ask what kind of store it is. Only the name you typed is sent: no amounts, cards or wallet. Answers are saved on your computer, in ~/.earnrate/merchant-cache.json, so the same name isn't sent twice. Anthropic handles the request under your agreement with them.

Downloading earnrate

When you install earnrate, npm and GitHub see the download, as they do for any package, under their own privacy policies. I can see npm's public download counts, but nothing about who downloaded it.

This website

earnrate.dev is a static site with no cookies and no server code. It counts visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, a small script that records which page you opened, the site that linked you here, how fast the page loaded, and your country, browser and device type. It sets no cookies and doesn't recognize you from one visit to the next. Cloudflare hosts the site and, like any host, handles your IP address and request details to serve the page and block abuse. Its dashboard shows me aggregate counts, not who visited. There's no server code and no logging turned on.

Email

Mail to hello@earnrate.dev or licensing@earnrate.dev is forwarded to my personal inbox. I use it only to reply to you, and I don't add anyone to a mailing list.

Changes

If any of this changes, for example if earnrate ever offers a hosted version, this page will say so first, with a new date.
