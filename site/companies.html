earnrate

For companies

earnrate's card data is free for personal use. If you're building a card app, an agent wallet or an assistant and want to use it in your product, you can license it.

What it is

Rules for U.S. credit cards from issuers, read from each issuer's own pages. Every rule quotes the page it came from. Every card records when it was last checked (last_verified), who checked it (verified_by) and the pages it was checked against (source_urls).

The data is versioned. Each release has an entry in the change log, with the old and new value and the issuer page behind each change.

What you can license

  • Card rules. Earn rates by category, store and how you pay; spending caps and when they reset; rotating quarterly categories with their dates and activation deadlines; bonus categories the cardholder picks; memberships a rate depends on, like Amazon Prime or Costco; annual and foreign transaction fees; spend bonuses; first-year rates for new accounts; statement credits at named stores, with their amounts, periods and enrollment; and welcome offers with their terms.
  • The merchant directory. About stores, each with its category, other names and web addresses, the merchant category code it usually posts with, which card networks it takes where that's known, and whether buying in store or online changes the category.
  • Updates from the weekly source check. Every Monday, a script loads each issuer page the cards cite, more than once, and checks that every rule's quoted text is still there. It also flags new lines on a page that state an earning rate, and welcome offers that show on some visits but not others. A flagged card is re-read against the issuer's pages before its data changes.

What's free

The code is MIT licensed. Anyone can use, change and ship the engine, including in a commercial product. earnrate.dev, the command line and the Claude connector are free for people to use.

The card data under data/ is CC BY-NC 4.0: free for personal, noncommercial use. Commercial use needs a license.

Why you can trust it

Card rules come only from the issuer's own pages, never from third-party datasets, review sites or blogs. Every rule carries a short quote from the page it came from, and the tests fail if one is missing.

There are no affiliate links, apply links or sponsored placements, and there never will be. A ranking depends only on the issuer's terms and the user's own cards and settings. A license doesn't change that: nobody can pay to move a card up.
